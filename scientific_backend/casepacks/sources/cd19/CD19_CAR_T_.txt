Is therapy failure more consistent with loss of functional CD19 recognition, or with inadequate antigen-specific CAR-T activity, persistence or expansion?

H1: A CD19 sequence change creates an alternatively spliced isoform that removes or disrupts the CAR-recognised epitope. Variant-to-isoform association; junction/isoform evidence; reduced surface CD19 despite tumour-cell presence. Normal full-length, surface-accessible CD19 in relapse tumour cells. Targeted long-read RNA or junction assay plus surface-CD19 flow cytometry.
H2: RNA-binding regulation, including PTBP1-linked regulation, changes CD19 splicing without a causal CD19 sequence variant. Differential RNA-binding or perturbation evidence with concordant isoform change. No isoform change, or a sequence variant explains the measured isoform outcome. Perturb the nominated RNA-binding factor and measure CD19 isoforms and surface protein.
H3: CD19 remains functionally present, but the CAR-T product has poor antigen-specific activation, expansion or persistence. Weaker response under CD19 stimulation than appropriate matched controls; dysfunctional state programs. Robust antigen-specific activation and persistence with continued tumour growth. Donor-aware CD19-versus-non-target stimulation and serial CAR-T abundance/function assay.
H4: Relapse is driven by CAR-T exhaustion or suppressive microenvironment rather than antigen escape. Exhaustion/dysfunction signatures, poor expansion, inhibitory microenvironment evidence while CD19 is retained. Tumour cells lack functional CD19 recognition. Matched tumour/CAR-T co-culture with exhaustion and killing readouts.
H5: Apparent CD19 loss is caused by tumour purity, lineage composition, batch, or assay-identity error. Target signal tracks sample composition or technical factor rather than malignant cells. Cell-resolved tumour measurements show reproducible loss. Verify sample/assay metadata and quantify CD19 within annotated malignant cells.
