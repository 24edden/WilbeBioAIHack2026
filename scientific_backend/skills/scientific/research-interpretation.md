# Clear findings and a useful working answer

Explain the investigation to a scientist or study leader who has not read the traces. Start with the best-supported answer to the observed findings. If patient-level causation is unresolved, name the leading working explanation at the supported assay, construct or cohort scope, then state the specific missing causal link. Do not make all explanations sound equally supported merely because none is proven. Conversely, do not rank incomparable explanations or manufacture a numerical probability. A working explanation is a proposal for investigation; it does not silently change the accepted hypothesis ledger.

Use familiar words, short sentences and direct verbs. Each finding should say what was observed, what it suggests and what decision it changes. Retain only numbers essential to the comparison, with units and denominators. Define scientific shorthand at first use. Avoid long lists of qualifications: put the decisive caveat next to the claim it qualifies. Preserve distinctions between newly computed data, published findings, predictions and proposed experiments.

For the user-facing Working answer companion, write for a study leader. Technical detail already exists in the evidence and audit panels. Keep hashes, sequence IDs, provider request IDs, internal hypothesis labels, barcode counts and algorithm names out of the narrative; use their dedicated reference fields and expandable records. Include one or two decisive effect sizes with the essential experimental unit rather than every statistic. Describe biological ideas in words instead of H1/H2 labels. Explain the scientific takeaway from NVIDIA first, then one short sentence about the limitation. The model job's existence and data-format validation are provenance, not the scientific takeaway. Do not begin every sentence with a disclaimer. The separate specialist work products still retain rigorous methods, controls and full quantitative evidence.

Tell the decision story in order: the question, the discriminating analysis chosen, what its result added, and why the team continued or stopped. Give concise evidence-based rationale, not hidden model deliberation. A specialist summary should describe its contribution rather than recopy its whole handoff. Explain missing data, an unavailable analysis method, failed QC and an unperformed experiment separately.

For molecular work, identify exact sequences by trusted input ID, accession/version, sequence hash, length and biological role. A target isoform is not a binding-domain candidate. Report only validated returned coordinates and provider metrics. A structure picture is an illustration of an artifact, not independent evidence. Use supplied artifact audits to discuss identity, request comparability, regional confidence and alignment sensitivity; state when these audits were newly computed after the historical decision. Global confidence is not affinity, accuracy, a probability of a hypothesis or clinical efficacy. A large whole-chain displacement can arise from relative domain orientation; do not call it local unfolding without a suitable comparison.

Prepare the next molecular comparison from verified sequence inventory and the current scientific objective. Select inventory IDs rather than inventing amino-acid strings. Never infer target retention or fill an absent binder sequence from a different target construct. If a manual binder form is unsuitable, describe the already completed target-structure comparison and the appropriate next audit or functional experiment. Do not repeat inference just to fill the form. A model draft is not permission to submit it.

End with one concrete working proposal: what is likely happening at the supported scope, why that is the strongest current lead, the main alternative or caveat, and the next result that would change the proposal. The independent reviewer must repair both unsupported certainty and unjustified agnosticism. Preserve historical results, human-review status, original hypothesis ownership and actual model identity. Citations and versioned receipts support verification; they do not prove interpretation.
