# The Challenge

## Premise

Incredible work happens when you connect domain expertise with frontier technology.
Advances in coding agents are enabling scientists who have never coded to orchestrate
complex workflows, and helping builders expand into new domains. This weekend is about
seeing what happens when researchers, engineers, and builders work on **real life
sciences problems** with the latest frontier models and accelerated tools.

Interdisciplinary teams will use the latest advances from **OpenAI** and **NVIDIA** for
life sciences research, including:

- **GPT-Rosalind** (OpenAI)
- **BioNeMo Agent Toolkit** (NVIDIA)

Experts from OpenAI and NVIDIA will be on-site to teach these tools and mentor teams as
they turn a real drug discovery or biology problem into a **working workflow, benchmark,
or prototype**.

**Venue:** WilbeLABS' London laboratories (WestWorks Labs), including facility tours to
keep scientific context close to the technical work.

## Tracks

Teams work in **one of four tracks**, supported throughout the weekend by mentors, TAs,
and track leads from OpenAI and NVIDIA.

| # | Track | What it means |
|---|-------|---------------|
| 01 | **OSS Build** | Modernise or extend useful bioinformatics software: repackage legacy tools, remove brittle dependencies, improve testing and documentation. |
| 02 | **Orchestration** | Combine specialist models and deterministic tools into repeatable workflows with clear inputs, outputs, and human decision points. |
| 03 | **Benchmarking** | Make performance and failure modes visible on focused scientific tasks via representative tests, baselines, and robustness checks. |
| 04 | **Open Track** | Bring a real wet-lab, bioinformatics, or data-analysis problem and build the smallest useful prototype around it. |

## Who Should Apply

Teams are formed across computational and biological expertise, to build and learn together.

- **Builders** — people working at the intersection of AI and biology: computational
  biologists, AI researchers, or software engineers curious about biology.
- **Scientists** — researchers and wet-lab scientists using coding agents to answer
  biological questions, bringing experimental knowledge and real-world scientific
  constraints.

## Speakers

### Michael Bronstein
*Founding Scientific Director, Aithyra · Google DeepMind Professor of AI, University of Oxford*

Founding Scientific Director of Aithyra, Google DeepMind Professor of AI at Oxford, and
Honorary Professor at TU Wien and the University of Vienna. Previously a professor at
Imperial College London and Head of Graph Learning Research at Twitter, with visiting
appointments at Stanford, MIT, and Harvard. A pioneer in **geometric deep learning** and
**AI for protein design**; 200+ papers, 35+ patents, five ERC grants. Serial entrepreneur:
co-founded Fabula AI (acquired by Twitter) and Invision (acquired by Intel).

### Simon Kohl
*Founder & CEO, Latent Labs*

Computer scientist and AI researcher. Latent Labs is a frontier AI lab building generative
models that let biotech and pharma companies design proteins and therapeutics. Previously
started and co-led **DeepMind's protein design team** and was a senior research scientist
on the Nobel Prize-winning **AlphaFold2** project. At Latent Labs he is advancing
generative biology through **Latent-X** and **Latent-X2**, which generate novel proteins
and high-affinity antibodies with drug-like properties. The company has raised $50M,
co-led by Radical Ventures and Sofinnova Partners.

### Anna Gogleva
*Senior Director, Machine Learning, Relation Therapeutics*

Leads research on **AI reasoning systems for drug discovery** at Relation Therapeutics:
how AI agents form, refine, and test scientific hypotheses — and, more crucially, how
those reasoning processes **fail**. Recent work includes **Tiny Moves**, a game-based
formalism for incremental editing of biological hypotheses. Background spans biology,
computational biology, and machine learning, with ML work in drug discovery at AstraZeneca
and Owkin, including graph-based methods for target identification and mechanistic
reasoning.
